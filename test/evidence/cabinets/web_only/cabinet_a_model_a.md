ANSWER:

Yes—there are several important problems here, and I would not add valerian yet. Because you take warfarin, arrange a same-day review with your anticoagulation clinic, prescriber, or pharmacist. Give them the exact product names, doses, and how often you take every supplement. Do not stop warfarin or change any prescription medicine yourself.

The biggest concerns are:

- **St John’s wort + sertraline:** This can cause too much serotonin, which can be life-threatening. Warning signs include agitation or confusion, sweating, diarrhea, fever, a fast heartbeat, tremor, muscle stiffness, or jerking movements. If these are happening, seek emergency care now.
- **St John’s wort + warfarin:** St John’s wort can make warfarin less effective and increase the chance of a clot. When St John’s wort is stopped, warfarin’s effect can then rise and cause bleeding. Ask the warfarin clinic today how to discontinue it and when to check your INR; do not simply adjust either product on your own.
- **Ginkgo + warfarin:** Ginkgo may increase the risk of serious bleeding. Sertraline also adds to warfarin’s bleeding risk. Get urgent medical help for bleeding that will not stop, vomiting or coughing blood, black/tarry or bloody stool, red or dark-brown urine, a severe sudden headache, unusual weakness, or a significant fall or head injury.
- **Red yeast rice + atorvastatin:** Red yeast rice may contain monacolin K, which is essentially the statin drug lovastatin. Taking it with atorvastatin can duplicate statin effects and raise the risk of muscle, kidney, and liver injury. Contact a clinician promptly for unexplained muscle pain or weakness, especially with fever or dark urine, or for yellow skin/eyes.
- **Calcium or iron + levothyroxine:** Both can prevent levothyroxine from being absorbed properly. Take levothyroxine consistently on an empty stomach, usually 30–60 minutes before breakfast, and keep calcium and iron at least 4 hours away from it. Iron and calcium can also interfere with each other’s absorption, so ask the pharmacist to help make a dosing schedule.
- **Ashwagandha + levothyroxine:** Ashwagandha may affect thyroid hormone and may interact with thyroid medicine. It can also cause drowsiness and has rarely been linked to liver injury. It should be reviewed rather than assumed safe.
- **Fish oil + warfarin:** Evidence does not show a general increase in bleeding from ordinary omega-3 use, but warfarin guidance still recommends that fish-oil use or dose changes be discussed with the anticoagulation team. High-dose purified EPA may modestly increase bleeding. Keep the dose and product unchanged until reviewed.
- **Vitamin D:** At an ordinary recommended dose, this is not an obvious major interaction in this list, but the dose and reason for taking it still matter. Calcium and iron should also be used only when there is a reason and an appropriate dose.

Some prescribed combinations here—such as warfarin with sertraline or levothyroxine—can be used when a clinician knows about them and monitors the INR. The risk is especially high when supplements are added, removed, or changed without the anticoagulation team knowing. After any advised change, ask when your next INR should be checked.

I would **not start valerian now**. Its benefit for insomnia has not been demonstrated consistently, long-term safety is uncertain, and it can cause drowsiness. Combining it with ashwagandha may make sedation worse, and rare liver injury has been reported with both products. Valerian should not be combined with alcohol or other sedatives. Ask your clinician to review the cause of the sleep problem and your full medication list first.

Until that review: take your prescriptions exactly as directed, do not add valerian or any other supplement, avoid changing supplement doses on your own, and contact the warfarin clinic/pharmacist today—particularly about St John’s wort, ginkgo, and red yeast rice.
