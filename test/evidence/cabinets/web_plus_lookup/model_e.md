Taking them together significantly increases the risk of a rare but serious condition called "serotonin syndrome," which can cause agitation, tremors, high fever, and rapid heart rate. Because of this, combining them is strongly advised against.

This means it can cause your body to process and eliminate warfarin and atorvastatin much faster than normal, reducing their effectiveness in your body. The EMA lists using St. John's Wort alongside coumarin-type anticoagulants (like warfarin) as a contraindication.
*   **With Levothyroxine:** No specific interactions between St. John's Wort and levothyroxine were noted in the reviewed sources.

Because you are already taking a prescription statin (atorvastatin), taking Red Yeast Rice on top of it can increase your risk of statin-related side effects, particularly severe muscle toxicity (rhabdomyolysis) and liver issues. It is strongly advised not to consume Red Yeast Rice if you are on cholesterol-lowering medication.

**Calcium, Iron, and Vitamin D**
*   **Calcium and Iron with Levothyroxine:** Both calcium (especially calcium carbonate) and iron supplements can physically bind to levothyroxine in your stomach, preventing your body from absorbing the thyroid medication. This can make your levothyroxine less effective. The standard recommendation is to take levothyroxine at least 4 hours apart from any calcium or iron supplements. 
*   **Vitamin D with Atorvastatin:** While vitamin D and calcium are routinely taken together safely, high intakes of vitamin D (from supplements) might compete with the same enzymes that process statins, potentially reducing the potency of your atorvastatin.

**Garlic**
*   **With Clopidogrel (Blood Thinner):** This is an important interaction. Clopidogrel is an anti-platelet medication (a blood thinner) used to prevent blood clots. Garlic also possesses antithrombotic (anti-platelet) activity. Taking garlic supplements while on clopidogrel can increase your bleeding times and overall risk of bleeding. **Iron, Vitamin C, and Zinc**
*   **Iron with Omeprazole:** Omeprazole is a proton pump inhibitor that reduces stomach acid. Because stomach acid is required to absorb nonheme iron (the type of iron usually found in supplements), taking omeprazole can reduce how much iron your body absorbs, making your iron supplement less effective. 
*   **Iron with Vitamin C:** Vitamin C actually *enhances* the absorption of iron. In healthy individuals, taking them together is fine (and often recommended if you are trying to boost iron levels). However, if you have a condition that causes iron overload (like hemochromatosis), taking high doses of Vitamin C with iron could result in absorbing too much iron and causing tissue damage.
*   **Iron with Zinc:** High doses of elemental iron (25 mg or more) taken at the exact same time as a zinc supplement can reduce the amount of zinc your body absorbs. It is generally recommended to take mineral supplements like iron and zinc a few hours apart, and take them with food.

Like valerian and passionflower, kava can potentiate (increase the strength of) centrally acting drugs, including sedatives like zolpidem. Combining kava with zolpidem, valerian, and passionflower increases the risk of excessive sedation and motor impairment. 
*   **Driving Warning:** Kava can adversely affect motor reflexes and the ability to drive or operate heavy machinery.
