# 1. Warfarin and ginkgo

ANSWER: Do not start ginkgo on your own while taking warfarin. The file contains mixed evidence: studies cited there did not find an interaction or changes in clotting measures, but other cited sources say ginkgo may increase bleeding risk or add to warfarin's anticoagulant effect. One source says not to use ginkgo with blood thinners, and another advises monitoring when ginkgo is started, changed, stopped, or switched. Discuss it with your doctor before use and arrange the recommended monitoring if they advise taking it.

# 3. Ginkgo, St John's wort, zinc, and warfarin

ANSWER: This combination needs careful review before you continue it. For ginkgo with warfarin, the file contains mixed evidence: cited studies did not find an interaction, but other sources say ginkgo may increase bleeding risk, and one says not to use it with blood thinners. Monitoring is advised when starting or stopping ginkgo, changing its dose, or changing products.

For St John's wort, the file says concomitant use with coumarin-type anticoagulants is contraindicated when the daily hyperforin dose is above 1 mg. Above that level, hyperforin can induce drug-metabolizing enzymes and speed the elimination of other drugs, decreasing their blood concentrations. For daily hyperforin intake of 1 mg or less for no longer than two weeks, the file reports no clinically relevant interactions for drugs handled by the listed pathways.

Take zinc with food and a few hours before or after warfarin and the other health products. Given the contraindication and the competing concerns about warfarin's effect, consult your doctor or pharmacist about the entire combination rather than adjusting it yourself.
